Guangyuan Wang 汪光远

Hello! I earned my double bachelor’s degrees in computer science and mathematics with highest honours at McGill University and Mila – Québec Artificial Intelligence Institute, where I did research on reinforcement learning and optimization theory. I was very fortunate to be advised by Prof. Doina Precup in the Reasoning and Learning Lab and Prof. Elliot Paquette in the Montréal Machine Learning and Optimization Group.

Previously, I worked on self-improving coding agents and automated R&D at Princeton with Prof. Mengdi Wang and Prof. Shilong Liu. I have also worked with Prof. Andrea Zanette at Carnegie Mellon, Prof. Anima Anandkumar at Caltech, and Prof. Junwei Lu at Harvard.

Research Interests

My research centers on efficient agentic reinforcement learning and recursive self-improvement: where RL should constrain an LLM’s token-level updates, when agents should refresh attention to reason over long horizons cheaply, and how models and harnesses can co-evolve into safe, reliable, fast, and self-improving coding agents, via model-fitted edit interfaces, transferable harness revisions, and lessons distilled from their own repairs.

I am especially interested in applying AI agents to scientific discovery and automated R&D, and I complement this with optimization theory that provably characterizes the training dynamics of LLM optimizers.

Broadly, I aim to build a principled understanding of agentic reasoning systems and to advance their intelligence. To this end, I am currently excited about several directions, including scaling reinforcement learning for agents, model architectures built for long-horizon agents, and continual learning that keeps models plastic.

Feel free to reach me if you would like to discuss research.

Publications and Preprints

  1. When Should an Agent Refresh? Adaptive Index Reuse for Efficient Long-Horizon Agentic Reasoning

    Guangyuan Wang, Yifan Zhang, Shilong Liu

    Preprint. Under review, NAACL 2027

    • Paper

    Building on IndexCache’s cross-layer index reuse for sparse-attention models such as GLM, we introduce AIR (Adaptive Index Reuse), which uses reinforcement learning to decide when a long-horizon language agent should refresh its sparse-attention selections, improving both success and speed at the same compute budget on coding, terminal and tool-use benchmarks.

  2. AIChipBench: Benchmarking LLM Agents for Automated AI Accelerator Design

    Guangyuan Wang, Chengran Li, Minchen Luo, Jichen Feng, Mengdi Wang†, Yifan Zhang†

    Preprint. Under review, ICLR 2027

    • Paper

    We introduce AIChipBench, a benchmark that tests whether LLM coding agents can turn model specifications into working AI accelerator designs, with independent held-out checks that catch failures public tests miss.

  3. TAME: Transfer-Aware Model-Harness Co-Evolution through Learned Harness Revisions

    Guangyuan Wang, Yifan Zhang, Shilong Liu

    Preprint. Under review, ICLR 2027

    • Paper

    We show that the harness that helps an agent most during training does not always leave the strongest model, and introduce TAME, which trains a feedback agent with reinforcement learning to revise the training harness for gains that transfer to independent deployment harnesses.

  4. Safe, Self-Evolving, and Swift Coding Agent

    Guangyuan Wang, Wei Deng, Langlin Wan, Yinling Zhang, Shilong Liu

    Preprint, in preparation

    • Webpage
    • Code

    We present S-Code, an open-source coding harness whose self-evolution layer turns observed repairs into short, auditable lessons that later sessions and other agents can safely reuse, without changing model weights or agent code.

  5. Physics of Coding Agents: Fit, Not Format at the Edit Interface

    Langlin Wan, Zhixuan Liang, Guangyuan Wang, Shilong Liu

    Preprint. Under review, FSE 2027

    • Paper
    • Code
    • Webpage

    We show that a coding agent’s edit interface works best when it asks for the forms a model writes most reliably: task success hides large differences in cost and failure modes across interfaces, which motivates Verified Selection, a new edit interface built on this fit.

  6. Learning Where to Constrain: Meta-Learned Trust Allocation for LLM RL Reasoning

    Guangyuan Wang, Padideh Nouri, Doina Precup

    Math Reasoning and AI Workshop, NeurIPS 2026

    • Paper

    We introduce Meta-TA, which meta-learns where an RL trust region should constrain an LLM’s token-level updates during reasoning, replacing hand-set allocations with one learned from feedback.

  7. SS-ESOAP: Self-Scaled Adaptive Preconditioning for Physics-Informed Learning

    Guangyuan Wang, Mads Toftrup, Sebastian Loeschcke, Yixuan Wang, Anima Anandkumar

    Preprint, 2026. Under review at JMLR

    • Paper
    • Code

    We introduce SS-ESOAP, a principled self-scaled Kronecker-factored preconditioner for physics-informed neural networks whose scaling factor provably solves a secant-matching problem, reaching lower residuals than first-order and SOAP-style optimizers on most PDE benchmarks.

  8. Phases of Muon: When Muon Eclipses SignSGD

    Elliot Paquette, Noah Marshall, Lucas Benigni, Guangyuan Wang, Atish Agarwala, Courtney Paquette

    NeurIPS 2026

    • Paper
    • Code
    • Webpage

    We provably characterize the high-dimensional training dynamics of stochastic Muon and SignSGD, deriving explicit deterministic dynamics and a phase diagram of when each optimizer wins, which shows why replacing Adam with Muon is not trivial even from a pure optimization perspective.

  9. High-Dimensional Isotropic Scaling Dynamics of Muon and SGD

    Guangyuan Wang, Elliot Paquette†, Atish Agarwala†

    Optimization for Machine Learning Workshop, NeurIPS 2025

    • Paper
    • Code
    • Webpage

    We derive exact risk recursions for Muon and SGD in high-dimensional matrix linear regression, giving principled rules for scaling batch size with dimension and suggesting that Muon’s default normalization may not preserve its nonlinear behavior at scale.

  10. Langevin Soft Actor-Critic: Efficient Exploration through Uncertainty-Driven Critic Learning

    Haque Ishfaq*, Guangyuan Wang*, Sami Nur Islam, Doina Precup

    ICLR 2025

    • Paper
    • Code

    We introduce LSAC, an actor-critic algorithm that explores through the uncertainty of a distributional critic learned with Langevin Monte Carlo, aided by parallel tempering and diffusion-generated actions, bringing Thompson-sampling-style exploration to continuous control.

More papers in preparation

  1. Can Language Models Learn Forever? Stationarity and Plasticity Under Continual Pretraining

    Guangyuan Wang, Guanning Zeng, Yuda Song, Andrea Zanette

    Preprint, in preparation

    We study when a long-trained language model is still worth continuing on new data, separating how well it holds onto its source corpus (stationarity) from how readily it can still learn (plasticity), and find that weight decay decides whether age slows learning down or speeds it up.

  2. Inference on Ceilings: Chandelier Debiasing for Optimal Policy Value with Continuous Treatments under Preference Data

    Guangyuan Wang, Junwei Lu

    Preprint, in preparation

    We develop principled pointwise uncertainty quantification for rewards learned from preference comparisons under a Bradley–Terry model, with a Green-function debiasing correction whose estimator provably attains oracle rates and asymptotic normality.

  3. ReflectAgents: Teaching Multimodal Agents to Say No

    Bo Gao, Guangyuan Wang, Guanning Zeng, Andrea Zanette

    Preprint, in preparation

    We study when reflection is worth its cost for tool-using vision–language agents, defining its value against a matched-compute continuation and using that value to train agents that reflect only when it helps.

  4. Solving Reflected Diffusion Models: A Physics-Informed Approach

    Liyuan Xu*, Guangyuan Wang*, Junwei Lu

    Preprint, in preparation

    We build a physics-informed score estimator for reflected diffusion models with a provable dimension-free, uniform-in-time error bound, and show that enforcing the Fokker–Planck boundary condition gives competitive likelihoods and efficient sampling.
